One box finds everything. Commands, the file's and the project's symbols, files and text, each in a section with its best few, as JetBrains' Search Everywhere and the screenshot have it. A scope chip narrows it to one, or its sign typed first does: > commands, @ symbols, / text. : goes to a line, as now.
Ctrl+P opens it on Files, as now, and Ctrl+Shift+P on Commands, each with the chip lit, so nothing anyone has learned changes. Clicking Everything, or Backspace on an empty box in a scope, widens it.
Each row says what it is. A command has its keys on the right, and a short line under its name where it has one. The letters that matched are in the accent colour. A symbol has its kind's letter in its colour, what it's in, its file and line. A file has its icon and folder. Text has the line, its match tinted, and where it is.
The › on a command opens what it can take (Fold at a level, a theme's list) where there's more, as VS Code's settings gear does. Ctrl+Enter opens a file or symbol to the side. Tab moves through the scopes; Shift+Tab goes back.
Light on memory and CPU. Commands, open files' symbols and file names are in memory already, and come at once. The project's symbols come from the language plugin, and text from the search merlin already runs. Both are asked for a moment after typing stops, and fill their sections when they come; a section that's still searching says so.
Sections take the room they need: four commands, three symbols, two files and one text match at most with Everything, and a "more" link where there are more. A section with nothing doesn't show.
